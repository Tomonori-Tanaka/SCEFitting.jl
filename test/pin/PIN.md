# The pin tier

Byte-level **change detectors** over the SALC chain. Run them with

```bash
julia --project=test/pin -t 4 test/pin/runtests.jl
julia --project=test/pin -t 1 test/pin/runtests.jl     # both, every time
```

and recapture with `PIN_DATE=YYYY-MM-DD PIN_COMMIT=$(git rev-parse --short HEAD)
julia --project=test/pin -t 4 test/pin/capture.jl` — **manually, and only under
the rules at the bottom of this file.**

## What a pin is, and what it is not

Every expected value in `pins/*.toml` was produced by this package. A green run
says *the output has not moved since capture* — nothing about whether it was
right in the first place. This tier is the standing exception to the repository's
test-oracle rule, and it carries the label the rule demands.

The correctness side of the same code is `test/unit/test_normalization.jl`: four
closed forms (a Heisenberg constant, the spherical addition theorem, a
Clebsch–Gordan completeness sum, and a member-multiplicity identity) that are
geometry, not captured output, and that therefore never move. **Read the two
together.** A pin that goes red while those stay green is a change; a pin that
goes red *because* those went red is a bug.

## Why the tier exists

The stretch of the numeric chain from the Reynolds projector through
`_canonical_basis`, `_canonicalize_members`, `folded` and `evaluate_salc` had no
owner. Everything that reached it was gauge- or scale-invariant, so its output
could move by a constant factor without a single test going red (measured
2026-08-21: doubling every `folded` tensor left the orbit-sum invariance check
and a member-resolved invariance check green at 1e-14).

## The layers

The runner reports **which layer moved**, because that is what decides what you
are allowed to do about it.

| layer | content | strictness | recapture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L0** structure | `n_salcs`, the pure-spin key components (`body`, `orbit_id`, `ls`, `Lf`, `block` — `L_S` and `decors` are derived on a pure-spin key, `L_S ≡ Lf` and `decors ≡ spin_decors(ls)`, and that relation is guarded by `test_persist.jl`'s v4 back-read, not by this pin), each member's `atoms` / `shifts`, each term's slot ranks and tensor shape | **exact on every platform** (integers and labels only) | forbidden |
| **L0′** coarse mask | the sign/support pattern of `folded` at `eps = 5e-6` | **exact on every platform** | forbidden |
| **L1** values | every `folded` entry, as the raw IEEE-754 bit pattern in hex | exact on the capture platform, `rtol = 1e-12` elsewhere | conditional |
| **L2** physics | `‖X_E‖_F²`, `r2`, the fitted `coef`, and held-out predicted energies | exact on the capture platform, loose tolerance elsewhere | conditional |

The **pointed (moment) roster** carries the same four layers with three
differences, because it is a different basis type with different design rows:
the L0 key prints the mark-flagged decoration multiset (`m1,1,1`) rather than
`spin_ls`, which drops a rank-0 mark entirely and would print the same string
for two different labels; L0 additionally carries the
`moment_resolvability` verdict (`vanishing`, `rank`), since which columns
vanish on the cell is structure; and L2 reports `rmse_moment` in place of `r2`
and held-out `predict_moment` values in place of energies.

**L0 is a precondition, not just a layer.** L0′ and L1 are compared by position;
if the structure moved, those two are comparing unrelated numbers, so the runner
stops after L0 for that fixture rather than emit a hundred meaningless failures.

**The L0′ threshold is measured, not chosen.** Over the six-crystal parity sweep
of 2026-08-21 (12,040 tensor entries): 7,728 are exactly zero, and the smallest
surviving nonzero magnitude is **0.239**. The builder prunes at `1e-10`, so the
gap between "pruned to zero" and "smallest survivor" is **9.38 decades**, and
`eps` sits at its geometric mean, `4.9e-6`, rounded to `5e-6`. Nothing lands
within four decades of the threshold in either direction, which is what makes L0′
exact across platforms: no rounding difference two machines could have can move
an entry across it.

**Why L1 is not declared portable.** The chain runs through `eigen` on the
Reynolds projector and through BLAS in the fold, and those kernels are
architecture-specific. Whether the values are in fact bitwise identical on
x86_64-linux and aarch64-darwin is **unmeasured**; the honest rule until it is
measured is "exact where it was captured, a tolerance elsewhere", and each pin
records its capture platform in `[meta]`. Byte-stability across **thread counts**
*is* measured — the pins are captured at `-t 4` and match exactly at `-t 1`,
which is why both are run.

**L2 is gauge-dependent on purpose.** `coef` is one representative of a solution
that is not unique once columns are frozen, so a legitimate change to the gauge
convention trips L2 and only L2. That is a signal to read, not a bug to hide.
Its targets are a seeded random vector rather than energies generated by pushing
a coefficient vector through this same basis: that round trip cancels, and a
uniform rescale of the whole basis would leave `coef` and `r2` numerically
identical (measured — only `design_frob2` and `heldout` moved).

**What no layer here covers.** Whether the member set is *complete* (a builder
that dropped an orbit outright would be pinned in its dropped state at the next
recapture — L0 catches the change, not the wrongness), and anything about a
fixture not in the list. `fixtures.jl` names what each of the five crystals is
there for.

## Recapture rules

A pin tier's failure mode is that someone re-runs `capture.jl` to make the suite
green. These rules exist to make that a decision rather than a reflex. **There is
no mechanical enforcement** — no branch protection, no pin-only-commit job — so
the runner printing the layer and the rule number is the whole mechanism.

1. **Diagnose first.** The runner prints which of L0 / L0′ / L1 / L2 moved.
2. **L0 or L0′ moved → treat it as a bug.** Recapture forbidden.
3. **L2 moved → a bug, or a design change.** Recapture only together with the
   `SPEC.md` / `CLAUDE.md` coupling-site updates and a re-measured real-data
   parity written into the `CHANGELOG.md`.
4. **L1 alone moved → recapture allowed**, provided (a) it is a pin-only commit,
   (b) its body names the hash of the change that moved it and records the
   measurement that L0 and L2 did not move, and (c) the reason is not "to make
   the suite green".
5. **Recapture happens on an explicit human instruction only.** An agent reports
   the layer verdict and stops there.
6. **No fingerprints or hashes in a pin** — `hash` is Julia-version-dependent,
   and a hash localises nothing when it breaks.
7. **Before recapturing, confirm `test/unit/test_normalization.jl` is green.**
   Those closed forms are geometry; if they moved, the recapture would be
   pinning a wrong answer.

## Files

| file | role |
|---|---|
| `fixtures.jl` | the five pure-spin crystals and the pointed roster, as raw data, with what each one is for |
| `payload.jl` | **one** traversal, used by both capture and check, so their orders cannot drift |
| `capture.jl` | regenerates `pins/*.toml`; never run by the suite. `PIN_ONLY=id1,id2` limits it to those fixtures — without it every pin is rewritten, including ones the change under way does not touch, and a rewritten pin detects nothing |
| `runtests.jl` | the checker and the layer verdict |
| `pins/*.toml` | the captured state, one file per fixture |

## The pointed fixture

`B2_FeRh_pointed` — a **2×2×2 supercell** of B2 FeRh, Fe marked and Rh not,
`lmax_env = [1, 1]`, `lmax_mark = 1`, `lsum = 2`, star radius admitting only the
Fe–Rh nearest-neighbour bonds.

The supercell is not a stylistic choice. On a primitive cell every neighbour is
reachable through several minimum images; the pointed star enumeration keeps
those tied instances (deliberately — see `_pointed_star_candidates`), and
`moment_resolvability` then refuses the basis outright with
`UnclassifiableBasis`. Measured on the 2-atom bcc Fe, the 2-atom B2 FeRh and the
8-atom rock-salt MnO cells: all three refuse. 2×2×2 is the smallest cell in this
roster whose nearest-neighbour stars are untied.

The narrow star radius is what keeps the payload comparable in size to the
pure-spin pins. A pointed member count carries a factor `N!` per instance, so
widening the star multiplies the `folded` dump by an order of magnitude without
adding structure: at `lmax_env = [2]` on a 27-atom simple-cubic cell the same
roster would carry 1.2 million tensor entries. As captured this fixture holds
13 SALCs, 968 members and 8,648 tensor entries — the largest pin in the tier,
and deliberately the only pointed one.
